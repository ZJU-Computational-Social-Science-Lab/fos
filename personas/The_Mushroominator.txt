> **[r/paint]**  Assuming that your 'white' tank is actually just thick polyethylene and not opaque white, your contractor is correct that the penetrating light will encourage algae growth.    This is also true:   Black gets hotter, has a greater dynamic movement throughout a heating/cooling cycle (day/night), and softens the plastic from heat- allowing the contents to bulge.    This extra movement will compromise your paint job- cracking, checking and wrinkling will be a problem if you leave it painted black.  Black paints also suffer the most from UV exposure. 

I have polyethylene fish tanks outside for aquaponics.   Algae is a problem there as well.    My solution was to paint a black coat- or two if I could still see a flashlight shining through from the other side, and top it with a couple coats of  white.   The black serves as an effective light blocker and the white topcoat reflects the sun and discourages heat absorption.     Krylon and Valspar both have paint specifically for coating plastics , but you need only use them on the bottom coat. Subsequent coats are adhering to that bottom coat, not the plastic, so any exterior latex should work for a top coat.   UV exposure shouldn't be a large issue with most exterior latex paints from reputable manufacturers.
